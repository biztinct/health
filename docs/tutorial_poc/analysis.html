02
The three concepts

Option 1 — Cinematic Guided Journey

Watch, then understand

Central idea: learning as a visible, story-shaped path. The map turns “a pile of menus” into two lines with stations, dependencies and a destination; the player turns each screen into a narrated scene where animation carries meaning — spotlight means look here, the trace-dot means this rate becomes this payslip line, the morph means this is what your change does.

Removes classroom coaching by replacing the trainer’s projector walkthrough. Narration, camera movement and before/after do what a trainer’s pointing and talking does — on demand, at the learner’s pace, in their language.

Advantages

  • Best first impression; strongest “wow”.
  • Best at teaching causality (Setup → Pay Run tracing).
  • Lowest cognitive load; linear, so progress means something.
  • Cheap to keep accurate — steps anchor to data-coach selectors that already exist for pb_coach.

Disadvantages & risks

  • Passive — watching is not doing.
  • Completion becomes theatre if quizzes are shallow.
  • Long lessons compete with a payroll clerk’s patience.
  • Anchors break silently when screens change; needs anchor-lint in CI.
Complexity
Low–Medium. ~70% already exists in pb_coach (spotlight, tours, welcome modal, launcher). New: journey-map surface, autoplay player, trace/morph moments, quizzes, progress persistence.
Accessibility
Good — narration is text (aria-live), the player is keyboardable, reduced motion swaps animation for instant states. Spotlight must keep 4.5:1 contrast on the dimmed layer.
Localisation
Tour content moves from JS literals into translatable records — about a day of plumbing, after which Vietnamese is a content task.
Maintenance
Per-screen change touches 1–3 steps. The outline data model doubles as written documentation.
Best for
First-week users; the guided-trial demo world; anyone who answers “give me the complete journey”.

Option 2 — Safe Payroll Simulator

Learn by doing — safely

Central idea: payroll competence is judgement, not navigation. The simulator makes the decisions the curriculum: consequence previews before risky actions, seeded anomalies, misconception-recovery when the learner takes the tempting-but-wrong path, and a confidence score earned by choices.

Removes classroom coaching by replacing the supervised “first payroll with a senior watching over your shoulder”. The recovery dialogs are that senior colleague saying “let’s rethink that” — available forever, without embarrassment.

Advantages

  • Deepest learning — doing and failing safely.
  • Directly reduces real errors: the missions rehearse the top three (blind compute, blind accept, live-rate edit).
  • Confidence score is a readiness signal managers can trust.
  • Vietnamese-realistic data makes it feel like work, not a game.

Disadvantages & risks

  • Most expensive to build honestly — each mission is a state machine.
  • Divergence from real screens if built as replicas.
  • Seeded scenarios go stale unless generated.
  • A badly-tuned recovery dialog reads as patronising.
Payobook’s unfair advantage: pb_demo already provides a regenerable practice world with real screens, partial read-only security and a live June run. Missions can run on the real UI against demo data, which kills the replica-divergence risk entirely.
Complexity
Medium–High as a separate surface; Medium on top of pb_demo. New work: the mission engine (steps, validation, consequence interception, debrief) plus seeded anomalies in the demo generator.
Accessibility
Inherits the host app; consequence and recovery dialogs are standard modals; no timers by design.
Localisation
Mission copy is structured strings; the practice data in pb_demo is already bilingual.
Maintenance
Highest per-workflow cost. Keep missions few and canonical — five to eight total, not exhaustive.
Best for
Users about to run their first real payroll; managers certifying readiness; experienced payroll people who want to try rather than read.

Option 3 — Contextual AI Learning Companion

Ask anything, anywhere

Central idea: the tutor is ambient. Instead of “go learn”, the user asks from where they stand, and the answer arrives grounded in the current screen, their role and the current data — as guidance with pointing, steps, calculations and warnings, never as an action performed on their behalf.

Removes classroom coaching by replacing the post-training support channel (“quick question…”). It is the only concept that helps at the moment of need, forever — including long after onboarding.

Advantages

  • Zero-navigation help; personalises for free (screen + role + state are the context).
  • The “why is this number X” calc-explainer is a genuine support-ticket killer.
  • Bridges to the other concepts — “Show me” is a micro-journey, “Let me practise” hands off to the simulator.
  • Substrate exists: pb_payroll_ai_insights already has screen-context, intent classification and tour-launching.

Disadvantages & risks

  • An LLM answering payroll and legal questions needs hard guard-rails — grounding corpus, refusal styles, no invented rates.
  • Discoverability: a dock can be ignored.
  • Per-query cost.
  • A bad answer damages trust faster than no answer; bilingual quality needs review, not just translation.
Complexity
Medium. The dock, rich-answer blocks and highlight engine are prototype-proven here; the backend extends the existing PayAI engine with a learning-content retrieval layer and permission-aware answer filters.
Accessibility
Chat and blocks are screen-reader friendly; point-at highlights need a text fallback (“in the sidebar, under Setup”); a language switch must also switch any voice output.
Localisation
Answers are generated in the user’s language, but the grounding corpus must exist in Vietnamese — the same content as Option 1’s lessons, so it is a shared asset.
Maintenance
Lowest per-screen cost once grounded: content lives in one corpus; a new screen needs a context blurb and suggested questions.
Best for
Everyone after week one; experienced users with one-off questions; returning users; read-only roles needing explanation rather than training.

04
Recommendation

Build the staged hybrid: Journey → Companion → Simulator, on a single shared content spine. No single option removes the trainer alone. The Journey teaches the map, the Simulator builds the hands, the Companion answers the moment.

The prototype deliberately demonstrates the seams: companion answers link into lessons and missions, lesson outlines feed companion answers, and mission debriefs produce the same checklists the companion serves.

Why this order

  1. Journey first — it is the cheapest leap from what exists (pb_coach already ships spotlight tours, a launcher, and AI “Show me”), and it creates the content spine that both other options consume: the per-station what / why / when / prerequisites / mistakes plus the lesson steps. Nothing built here is throwaway.
  2. Companion second — PayAI already has screen-context and tour-launching. Adding the learning corpus and rich-answer blocks (steps with point-at, calculation tables, consequence warnings, role-aware refusals) converts existing plumbing into the support-ticket killer.
  3. Simulator last — the most expensive and most valuable after users know the map, and because running missions on the real UI over pb_demo data deserves the mission-engine investment to be done carefully, once.

What ships in the first release

Phase 1 — must include

  • Journey map for Pay Run and Setup
  • Four full lessons: Run Payroll, Pay Runs & approvals, Statutory, Formula Engine
  • Outlines for every remaining submenu
  • Quizzes and progress persistence
  • English / Vietnamese, reduced motion, resume

Can follow later

  • Autoplay narration voice
  • Confidence scoring
  • Celebration and motion polish
  • The remaining lessons

Technical foundations required

  • A pb.learn.content model — stations, lessons, steps, outlines, quizzes; translatable, exported to the frontend as JSON. Single source for the Journey, the Companion’s grounding, and mission copy.
  • An anchor registry — every data-coach selector named in content is checked by a CI script against the rendered templates, so screen changes fail loudly rather than silently.
  • Progress persistence per user (pb.learn.progress).
  • PayAI grounding retrieval over the content model.
  • A mission engine — an OWL service intercepting real actions inside the demo company.
Keeping it accurate as the app changes: content lives beside code in module data files; anchor-lint runs in CI; PRs touching Pay Run or Setup screens carry a content-owner review; and the Companion’s fallback answer explicitly admits when it does not know rather than guessing.

05
Phased roadmap

Phase 1 — Pay Run & Setup proof of value 3–4 weeks

Content model and anchor lint; journey map with four full lessons plus outlines; quizzes, progress, EN/VI. Ship to the demo world first — guided-trial users are the safest beta cohort.

Phase 2 — Remaining menus + Companion 3–4 weeks

People, Insights and Workforce stations; PayAI learning intents grounded in the content model; rich-answer blocks, point-at highlights and role-aware refusals; “Show me” and “Open lesson” bridges.

Phase 3 — Personalisation & Simulator 4–6 weeks

Starting modes; role-filtered paths; resume and re-teach (struggled twice → offer a simpler explanation plus a mission); the mission engine on pb_demo with five canonical missions; confidence scoring; manager view.

Phase 4 — Optimisation with behavioural data ongoing

Lesson-abandonment funnels; question clustering that becomes new content; English-versus-Vietnamese comprehension gaps; quiz-item analysis; support-ticket deflection tracking. Prune what nobody uses.

07
Assumptions & open questions

Amber numbers are open questions needing your decision; the rest are assumptions I made to keep moving.

  1. Menu scope. I took the active pb_sidebar items. Inactive children (Insurance Policies, Tax Tables under Statutory) are folded into the Statutory lesson. Please confirm.
  2. Setup’s position. The sidebar data places Setup between Pay Run and People (sequence 25); the prototype mirrors that.
  3. Statutory values. 8/17.5, 1.5/3, 1/1, ₫11m and ₫4.4m deductions and the 5% first band are practice values for the fictional company. Production content must read the live policy records — never hardcode.
  4. Delivery surface. I assumed the tutorial ships inside the existing OWL web client, not as a separate site; the prototype’s shell is a stand-in only.
  5. Open — should mission progress and confidence be visible to managers (HR analytics), or stay private to the learner? This changes the Phase 3 data model.
  6. Open — voice narration: PayAI already has voice. Is it worth it for factory-floor users on mobile? Currently deferred to Phase 3.
  7. Open — does the guided-trial demo world adopt Phase 1 immediately? Recommended — it is the safest audience and the highest sales value.

08
Integrating into the real app

What it concretely takes to turn the recommended concept into shipping code.

  1. New module pb_learn — models for content and progress; assets for the map surface and lesson player. The player is an evolution of pb_coach’s overlay: keep one engine, not two.
  2. Port the prototype content (data.js) into pb_learn/data/*.xml translatable records, with Vietnamese reviewed by a native payroll speaker.
  3. Extend the pb_coach overlay: autoplay bar, consequence and tip cards, trace and morph moments, quiz modal, progress wiring. Add the missing data-coach anchors on statutory and payslip lines.
  4. Extend pb_payroll_ai_insights: a learning intent; retrieval over pb_learn content; a rich-answer block renderer in ai_insight_chat.js (steps, calc and warning blocks as OWL components); a role-aware answer filter driven by res.groups.
  5. Mission engine (Phase 3): an OWL service scoped to the demo company that intercepts compute, approve and policy-write actions to inject consequence previews and validate steps; seeded anomalies added to the pb_demo generator.
  6. CI: an anchor-lint script comparing content anchors against template data-coach attributes.
