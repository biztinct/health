Where every channel actually stands

All eight channels — Zalo, ZNS, WhatsApp, Messenger, Telegram, Web Chat, Email, Calls — are built and tested. None are carrying real traffic yet. This page is the honest reason why, channel by channel, and the exact manual step that unblocks each one. Since the first edition, those manual steps are no longer a static checklist: the Go-Live Studio now walks an operator through every one of them.

Checked live on the server, 5 August 2026 Updated 5 August 2026 — Go-Live Studio shipped For business owner · platform operator Full technical record docs/strategy/handovers/channel-center-architecture.md
Software side: closed, all 8 channels. Live traffic: zero — every channel is waiting on a human approval or account outside Health19, not on more building. What changed: that human now has a guided console instead of a checklist.
They look alike on purpose — they are not the same job

Two consoles: one switches a channel on for everybody, the other connects one clinic

Both screens are guided, step-by-step and share the same visual language, which is deliberate — one house style for anything that asks a human to set something up. What separates them is whose account is being connected, and how many times it happens.

Go-Live Studio Register the provider application

The paperwork on Meta's, Zalo's, Google's and Microsoft's own developer sites: create the app, pass their review, paste back our addresses. This is what makes a channel offerable at all.

Who
The platform operator — your own team, system administrators only.
How often
Once per provider, for the whole platform. Four journeys total, ever.
Produces
One Platform Application row — an app id, a secret, a couple of configuration ids.
Where
Sidebar → Admin → Channel Go-Live
Channel Connection Center Connect this clinic's own account

A clinic signs in with their Zalo Official Account, their Facebook page, their mailbox — then Care Command proves the connection with automatic health checks before trusting it with real messages.

Who
Every clinic, self-service — no technical knowledge, no developer account.
How often
Once per clinic, per channel — and again for each extra account they run.
Produces
One connection per account, with its own inbox, checklist and health state.
Where
Sidebar → Care Command → Channels

→
AUTOMATIC The channel card stops saying "Not available yet" No code change, no redeploy — the card reads the same row the Studio just filled.
→
THEN · MANY TIMES Each clinic presses Connect in the Center They sign in on the provider's own page; we never see their password.
The short version

The Studio is the landlord getting the building wired to the grid — done once, by you. The Center is each tenant moving in and turning their own power on. Until the first is done, the second cannot start: that is exactly why every card on this page still reads Not available yet.

Shipped since the first edition of this page

The manual steps are now a guided journey

Everything on the provider to-do list below used to exist only as a static checklist on a developer-only screen, reachable by typing its address. It is now a full-screen guided studio: one journey per provider, deep links straight into the right page of the provider's console, copy buttons for every address they ask for, format-checked paste-back fields, and honest waiting states for the reviews that genuinely take weeks.

4guided journeys — Meta, Zalo, Google, Microsoft
22steps, each with its own instructions and console link
2steps that prove themselves live, while you watch
202automated tests passing on the server

Two moments verify themselves rather than asking you to trust a tick-box: pasting the Meta secret asks Meta to confirm it immediately and returns your own app's name, and pressing "Verify and save" in Meta's webhook dialog turns the milestone green in the Studio while the operator is still looking at it. Where no such proof exists — Google and Microsoft credentials, which are only ever tested on a real mailbox sign-in — the screen says so instead of showing a checkmark it hasn't earned.

A step can also be handed to someone else: if the person holding the Facebook business account isn't the person running Health19, that single step can be emailed to them as a private link that expires in fourteen days and can be revoked at any time. It carries the instructions and the addresses to paste — never a password or a secret, which only ever get typed by a signed-in operator.

One prerequisite before that last part works

Sending those hand-off links needs an outgoing mail server, and this server currently has none configured. The Studio fails honestly today — it tells the operator the message could not be sent and cancels the link rather than leaving a dead invitation behind — but delegation stays unusable until mail is set up.

Reading the status column

Four states

LIVE Connected, tested, carrying real messages right now.
BLOCKED — EXTERNAL STEP Code is complete and tested. Cannot switch on until a human finishes something on the provider's own site — an app review, a business verification, a signed contract.
NOT STARTED Nobody has begun the external step yet.
NEEDS ATTENTION Leftover test data or a known gap — not a channel failure, but worth a look.

The actual to-do list

Five providers, each unlocking one or more channels

Everything above collapses to five external relationships. None of them are Health19 development work — they are accounts, forms and reviews on someone else's website.

MetaBLOCKED
Unlocks WhatsApp + Messenger
  1. Create a Business-type app and complete Business Verification.
  2. Pass App Review for five permissions: whatsapp_business_management, whatsapp_business_messaging, pages_show_list, pages_messaging, pages_manage_metadata.
  3. Create an Embedded Signup configuration for WhatsApp and a Login for Business configuration for Messenger.
  4. Paste the redirect URI and both webhook URLs (generated on our side) into the app, with the verify token.
Business verification is the long pole — often 1–3 weeks, and can bounce back for more documents. Guided: 7 steps in the Studio, two of which verify themselves live.
ZaloBLOCKED
Unlocks Zalo chat + ZNS notifications
  1. Create an app on Zalo Developers and link it to the Official Account.
  2. Add our redirect URI to the app's OAuth settings.
  3. Set the single webhook URL Zalo allows per app — every tenant's OA shares it, routed by account id on our side.
ZNS needs one more thing on top: a funded ZCA account, then each message template individually reviewed by Zalo (2–3 days, per template). Guided: 5 steps in the Studio.
GoogleBLOCKED
Unlocks Email (Gmail)
  1. Create an OAuth 2.0 Client ID in Google Cloud Console and enable the Gmail API.
  2. Add our redirect URI to Authorised redirect URIs.
  3. Publish the consent screen — or list every clinic mailbox as a test user. An unpublished screen silently expires connections after 7 days.
  4. For full read/send access: a restricted-scope + CASA security assessment (4–12 weeks, a lab fee). A send-only version can ship sooner without it.
Google gives us no harmless way to test these credentials in advance — they are proven on the first real mailbox sign-in, and the Studio says so rather than showing a tick. Guided: 5 steps.
MicrosoftBLOCKED
Unlocks Email (Microsoft 365)
  1. Register an app in Microsoft Entra ID; add the Mail.Send, Mail.ReadWrite, IMAP.AccessAsUser.All and offline_access permissions.
  2. Add our redirect URI as a Web redirect URI.
  3. Create a client secret — Entra secrets expire, so the expiry date needs tracking.
Only one of Google or Microsoft is required to light the Email card — pick whichever the clinic's own mailboxes already run on. Guided: 5 steps, including the one everybody gets wrong (copy the secret's Value, not its Secret ID — it is shown only once).
VoIP24hBLOCKED
Unlocks Calls — receiving only
  1. Fetch VoIP24h's real API documentation from docs-sdk.voip24h.vn — geo-blocked to Vietnam IPs only.
  2. Reconcile it against the endpoints already built (they were written from public traces, never confirmed against the real spec).
  3. Get VoIP24h support to issue account credentials and confirm their webhook signature scheme.
Outbound calling was never in scope here — this channel only ever listens for call events VoIP24h sends us. No Studio journey, deliberately: there is no developer app to register, so a guided flow would be inventing work that doesn't exist. The Studio shows a plain card saying exactly that, and points at the Center.

Where a provider app actually gets entered

The operator console, and the expert door behind it

Once any one of the five provider relationships above is ready — an app id, a secret, a couple of configuration ids — the Go-Live Studio is where it goes, and filling it in is the whole activation: no code change, no redeploy. Underneath the Studio sits the original raw screen, Platform Applications, one row per provider. It survives as the expert escape hatch for anyone who already knows exactly which field they need, and the two can no longer disagree with each other: both now read the same single list of steps.

The Studio is reachable four ways — the sidebar leaf under Admin, a menu entry, a banner on the Channel Center that counts how many providers are ready, and a button on the raw form itself.

Previously flagged, now closed

The first edition of this page noted that the operator screen was reachable only by typing its address, with no sidebar link — fine for one operator, awkward for a second. That is fixed: Admin → Channel Go-Live is a normal sidebar entry, visible to system administrators and to nobody else.

Two small things, neither blocking

Known software gaps

  • Reply Templates can only be tagged for Zalo, Calls, Email, ZNS or "Any channel" — WhatsApp, Messenger, Telegram and Web Chat aren't offered yet, even though all four channels are otherwise live in the framework. A five-minute fix once someone needs a WhatsApp-specific canned reply.
  • "Add another account" — the button that lets one clinic run, say, a Hanoi Zalo account and a separate HCMC one — only appears once a channel has both a working first connection and its provider app is registered. Until the steps above are done, it stays hidden. It is not missing; it has nothing to attach a second account to yet.
