How every enquiry becomes a lead in the CRM

One page covering all the ways a person can reach us — web form, Google, phone, Zalo, Facebook — what each one delivers into the CRM today, what still has to be built, and exactly what the website developer needs to do.

Updated 29 July 2026 For business owner · website developer (Proima) · CRM team Full technical spec docs/strategy/website-crm-integration.md
Start here

The idea in four steps

Today, someone fills in a form on the website, an email lands in a staff mailbox, and a person re-types it into the CRM — or doesn't. Nobody can answer "how many leads did the Hanoi Google campaign produce last month?" without counting by hand.

The fix is to let the website talk directly to the CRM the moment a form is submitted, carrying the invisible context — which city, which ad, which campaign — along with the enquiry.

STEP 1 The visitor arrives Usually from a Google or Facebook ad. The ad quietly attaches a tracking code to the web address.
STEP 2 The website remembers That code, plus the campaign name and the page they landed on, is kept in the browser while they read.
STEP 3 They submit the form Name, phone, city choice — plus everything remembered in step 2 — is sent straight into the CRM, and the staff email still goes out as before.
STEP 4 The CRM decides New person → new lead. Someone who already enquired → added to their existing lead. Same form sent twice → ignored.
The one distinction that matters

Access to Google Analytics, Google Ads or Facebook Ads dashboards shows how many anonymous people clicked. It never creates a named lead in the CRM. Only the form connection (and, later, phone-call tracking) can do that. Everything below is labelled with which of the two it is.

Your question, answered directly

The form also sends an email — will we get two leads?

Short answer: no — and as of Phase W2, all three roads are guarded. A single form submission can travel down three different roads into the CRM. The first two were guarded by the original build; the third — the notification email — was a closed but unguarded door, and it now has its guard in place before anyone opens it.

Verified on the live CRM database on 29 July 2026.
How a duplicate could happen What stops it Status
The website sends the same submission twice (retry after a timeout, visitor double-clicks) Every submission carries a unique ticket number. The second arrival is recognised and returns the original lead instead of creating a new one. LIVE
The same person submits a form twice this week, or submits after phoning The CRM looks for an open lead with the same phone number (or email) and the same name. If it finds one, the new enquiry is added to that lead's history instead of becoming a second lead. LIVE
Two different people share one phone number (family) Deliberately creates two leads, cross-referenced to each other, and flags them for a human. Merging two real people by accident is far worse than an extra row. LIVE
The notification email also creates a lead The CRM's lead email addresses still cannot receive anything today (no mail domain, no mailbox connected) — and the guard is now built anyway: any incoming email carrying a ticket number the CRM already holds is attached to that existing lead instead of creating a new one. The door stays closed, but it is now safe to open. One condition: the website plugin must stamp the ticket number as the final line of the notification email — that requirement is in the developer steps below. LIVE
Built in W2
The two rules that keep it that way

1. Rule for the website developer (free): the form's notification email must go only to the staff mailboxes it goes to today. Never add a CRM address as a recipient, CC or BCC.

2. Rule for the website plugin (built into the CRM side already): the notification email must end with the line [web-lead:<ticket number>] — the same ticket number the direct connection carries, generated once so both see it. The CRM's guard recognises that line and files the email onto the existing lead instead of creating a second one.

For the website developer

Seven steps, in order

All work is on the WordPress side; the CRM never reaches into the website — the only connection is the website sending a message out to one address. The step-1 plugin is written once, as our own product: for pkgdvietuc.com Proima installs and wires it, and for every future clinic the work shrinks to installing the plugin and pasting two keys. Steps 1–3 deliver working leads; steps 4–7 add the reporting quality and legal cover.

Endpoint and credentials are already provisioned and waiting — ask the CRM team for the secret.

Add data-city="hanoi" or data-city="hcmc" to every tel: link, Zalo widget and Facebook link. Then fire these events in GTM: generate_lead on successful form send, click_to_call, zalo_click, messenger_click — each with the city and the page. Three rules that prevent painful mistakes

Always reuse the same submission_id when retrying — that is the entire duplicate defence. A new ID on a retry creates a second lead.
Save the submission locally before sending — if the send fails and nothing was saved, the enquiry is gone.
A spam rejection comes back as a success (rejected_spam), not an error. Mark it done and never retry it.

Making this sellable to other clinics

Turning the setup into a self-service connector

Until this phase, the connection was switched on the way a developer does it: someone logged into the server, created a service account and generated a secret by hand. That is now one screen. An administrator opens Website Connector in the CRM sidebar, presses one button for credentials, copies a ready-made configuration block for the website developer, edits the city mapping with validation, and runs a self-cleaning test — no server access, no developer. Chat channels stay in the Channel Connection Centre; the screen cross-links to it.

Built, tested and independently reviewed on the live server as phase W2.5. Every action is recorded on the record's own audit trail.

The Website Connector screen — live in the CRM sidebar since phase W2.5.
