0.Bottom line up front

Correction we are making first

"Compliant" was the wrong word and we are withdrawing it. The accurate statement is: the system implements HL7 FHIR R4 (4.0.1) and is conformant by construction and self-assessed; it has not been externally tested or certified by any body. Nobody has certified this system, and no HL7 conformance test suite has been run against it. The engineering underneath is real and verifiable — but the word "compliant" implies an external attestation that does not exist, and you should not carry that word into a submission to a Vietnamese authority.

The reassuring part, and it is material

Vietnam does not certify healthcare software products. There is no Vietnamese equivalent of the US ONC Health IT Certification Program. Under Circular 54/2017/TT-BYT the facility — not the vendor, not the app — determines its own IT-application level and reports it to its supervising health authority; under Circular 13/2025/TT-BYT the facility deploys electronic medical records and issues its own internal regulation governing them. The software is evidence in the facility's file, not a separately licensed object. So the approval you need is obtainable, and it does not depend on us holding a certificate that no Vietnamese authority issues.

3.Capability Statement — published, public, and machine-generated

Q. Can you provide the system's current Capability Statement? Is it published/accessible, or only available internally?

It is public, live, and requires no credentials. The client can fetch it right now:

curl https://care.biztinct.com/fhir/r4/metadata

This is the FHIR-specified conformance endpoint. It returns 200 with a complete CapabilityStatement resource. It contains no patient data, which is why it is safe to leave unauthenticated — and leaving it unauthenticated is what the specification expects.

3.1 Why this statement can be trusted more than a hand-written one

The CapabilityStatement is not maintained by hand. It is generated at runtime from the same serializer registry that actually serves the requests (health_fhir_core/capability.py, derived from serializers.REGISTRY). The resource list, the interaction list and the search-parameter tables are read out of the live code path.

The practical consequence for the client's drift question in §5: the declared capability cannot silently diverge from the implemented capability for resources and search parameters, because there is only one source of truth. If a developer adds a resource, it appears in the statement automatically; if they remove a search parameter, it disappears. This eliminates the most common category of conformance drift by construction rather than by discipline.

The known exception — operations declared separately from routes — is exactly the gap identified in §9, item G2.

4.2 What the internal process does not check — stated plainly

  • Semantic conformance. A resource can be structurally valid and still be clinically or semantically wrong. Nothing checks that our Encounter status mapping is the mapping a Vietnamese hospital would expect.
  • Search-parameter behaviour against the specification's expectations. We test that our search parameters do what we intended, not that a conformant client's queries behave as the FHIR search specification requires (modifiers, chaining, :missing, prefix semantics on all types).
  • Terminology binding validation. We do not verify that codes we emit are members of the ValueSets the specification binds those elements to.
  • Interoperability. Passing our own tests proves internal consistency. It does not prove that a third-party FHIR client can consume our output — that requires an actual third-party client, which is what a Connectathon or a bilateral test provides.
  • The tests are not run automatically. There is no CI pipeline. They run when an engineer runs them.

6.1 The basis on which we would defend a claim — and its exact limits

The engineering basis for saying something stronger than "designed toward" is real and it is threefold:

  1. It runs. The endpoint is live, publicly introspectable, and serves 21 resource types today. This is not a specification or a roadmap.
  2. It is validated against the official structure definitions. Every resource the system emits is constructed through the fhir.resources pydantic models generated from the HL7 R4/R4B StructureDefinitions. Malformed FHIR fails the build.
  3. It declares only what it does. The CapabilityStatement is code-generated, the facade rejects unsupported search parameters with a strict OperationOutcome rather than silently ignoring them, and truncation is declared rather than hidden. These are exactly the behaviours external conformance suites test for and where hand-built facades typically fail.
And the limits, which we should never blur

None of that is certification, and none of it is interoperability evidence. Structural validity ≠ semantic correctness ≠ a working interface with somebody else's system. Nobody outside our team has tested this, ever.

8.The Vietnam approval path — what is actually being approved

Q. When will this build be submitted to SYT/MOH? We have to record the assessing body, date and version number. Inspectors can order us to stop using an unauthorised system.

The most important correction in this document

Under Vietnamese law there is no mechanism by which a software build is "submitted to SYT/MOH" for approval, because Vietnam does not certify or license health IT products. The regulated object is the medical examination and treatment facility. The facility determines its own compliance position, issues its own decision, and reports to its supervising health authority. Our software is evidence inside that file. There is no separate vendor approval to obtain, and no queue to join.

This is good news for schedule, and it means the client's real risk is not "our app was never approved" — it is "our facility never completed its self-assessment and reporting obligations". That is what an inspector would find fault with, and it is entirely within the client's control.

8.1 The two instruments, and what each actually requires

InstrumentRegulated party & mechanismWhat must happen
Circular 54/2017/TT-BYT

Eight criteria groups (infrastructure; management software; HIS; RIS-PACS; LIS; non-functional; information security and safety; EMR) map to a level scale. If any part of the operation is classified as a bệnh viện (hospital), the 30 September 2025 deadline has already passed and the position needs to be regularised with the SYT immediately. If it is an "other facility" — the likely case for a home-care and clinic operator — the deadline is 31 December 2026, roughly five months from now, and the plan in §10 fits inside it with margin.

8.2 What FHIR does and does not buy you here

Neither circular mandates FHIR. Demonstrable FHIR R4 conformance is nonetheless genuinely useful in the file, for three reasons: it evidences the "interoperability / HIS interface readiness" criterion under 54/2017; it evidences that the EMR can be exported and exchanged rather than trapped in one vendor's database, which is the policy intent behind 13/2025; and it positions the client ahead of the VN Core IG becoming a national standard. It is a strengthener, not the gate.

8.4 Two risks larger than FHIR, which we are raising unprompted

R1 — Data residency

The environment we inspected runs on AWS ap-southeast-2 (Sydney, Australia). Vietnamese patients' health data — sensitive personal data — is therefore currently processed and stored offshore. For a healthcare SaaS operating in Vietnam this is, in our assessment, a materially higher-probability blocker than any FHIR question. We are not giving a legal opinion — this needs Vietnamese counsel, and it needs them before production go-live, not after. The engineering response, if localisation is required, is a migration to a Vietnam region or a licensed local provider; that is planning and downtime, not redevelopment.

R2 — Legally-valid digital signature

Circular 13/2025 turns on records being electronically authenticated. Our tamper-evident SHA-256 seal proves integrity but is not a signature issued by a licensed Vietnamese certification authority. Integration with VNPT-CA, Viettel-CA or an equivalent licensed CA should be treated as a prerequisite for the 13/2025 filing, not as a later enhancement.

10.Remediation programme — closing every gap

This section is the plan to take the register in §9 to zero. It is written to be executed and audited, not read: 23 work packages, each with a named owner, a specific change, a testable acceptance criterion, and a physical artefact that lands in the regulatory dossier.

10.1 The principle that makes "no gaps left" a truthful claim

An item leaves the register through one of exactly two doors, and there is no third state:

ExitWhat it requires
Closed by workThe defect is fixed, an automated test proves it stays fixed, and an artefact evidencing the fix exists in the dossier. Applies to G1–G5, G8–G12, G14–G17.
Closed by decisionThe item is a scope choice rather than a defect. It is written down, dated, assigned an owner, given an explicit trigger condition that would reopen it, and signed off by the accountable party. Applies to G13, G18 (conditionally), G20, and the roadmap items.

Why the second door is legitimate and not a dodge. An undecided absence is a gap: it means nobody has thought about it, and an assessor or a partner will read it as an oversight. A decided absence — recorded, justified, owned, with a stated condition under which it gets built — is a scope boundary, which every system has. The difference is entirely in whether the decision exists on paper with a name against it. What we will not do is leave anything in the third state of "we're aware of it".

One engineering principle governs the whole programme: mechanism over discipline. Where a control could be either a written rule or an automated gate, we build the gate. Every finding in §5.1 traced back to something that depended on a person remembering; none of the controls we add may depend on that.
