B. Writable dashboard targets (§4.3)
------------------------------------

Same session, before this persona owned anything:

    bi.dashboard.get_wizard_targets()  ->  []            (writable)
    bi.dashboard.search_read([],[...]) ->  7 dashboards  (readable)

after it saved one report of its own:

    {"writable_after_save": [{"id":445,"name":"AH3 QA Dashboard"}],
     "readable_count": 8,
     "readable": ["AH3 QA Dashboard","BI Usage Analytics",
                  "Báo cáo hoạt động điều hành","Facility Analytics",
                  "Monthly Revenue by Facility","Operations Overview",
                  "Revenue & Receivables","Visit Margin"]}

The wizard now offers the 1, not the 8. The predicate is the same one
`bi_dashboard.get_dashboard_data()` publishes as `can_edit`
(`biz_bi/models/bi_dashboard.py:213-216`); `test_ah3_03_wizard_targets`
asserts the two agree record by record.


C. What was NOT widened
-----------------------

* no `ir.model.access` row was added, changed or removed anywhere;
* no `ir.rule` was added, changed or removed anywhere;
* no group was granted to anybody by this phase;
* `nlq_chart` / `compose_report` still call `dataset.check_access('read')` as
  the REAL user before anything else happens — `test_ah3_02` proves the
  refusal fires before a provider is even resolved;
* the sudo is scoped to the `bi.ai.provider` recordset (`self` is never
  sudo'd), so `bi.ai._log`'s bookkeeping is byte-for-byte what it was.

Known, deliberate, and worth stating plainly: after §4.1 the *boolean*
"is an AI provider configured on this database?" is answerable by any
internal user who can reach `bi.ai.is_available` over RPC, where before they
got an AccessError. That is the intended trade — a capability probe must have
an answer for everyone (ledger §5.127b) — and it discloses one bit, not a row.
