How every enquiry becomes a lead in the CRM

One page covering all the ways a person can reach us — web form, Google, phone, Zalo, Facebook — what each one delivers into the CRM today, what still has to be built, and exactly what the website developer needs to do.

Updated 29 July 2026 For business owner · website developer (Proima) · CRM team Full technical spec docs/strategy/website-crm-integration.md
Start here

The idea in four steps

Today, someone fills in a form on the website, an email lands in a staff mailbox, and a person re-types it into the CRM — or doesn't. Nobody can answer "how many leads did the Hanoi Google campaign produce last month?" without counting by hand.

The fix is to let the website talk directly to the CRM the moment a form is submitted, carrying the invisible context — which city, which ad, which campaign — along with the enquiry.

STEP 1 The visitor arrives Usually from a Google or Facebook ad. The ad quietly attaches a tracking code to the web address.
STEP 2 The website remembers That code, plus the campaign name and the page they landed on, is kept in the browser while they read.
STEP 3 They submit the form Name, phone, city choice — plus everything remembered in step 2 — is sent straight into the CRM, and the staff email still goes out as before.
STEP 4 The CRM decides New person → new lead. Someone who already enquired → added to their existing lead. Same form sent twice → ignored.
The one distinction that matters

Access to Google Analytics, Google Ads or Facebook Ads dashboards shows how many anonymous people clicked. It never creates a named lead in the CRM. Only the form connection (and, later, phone-call tracking) can do that. Everything below is labelled with which of the two it is.

Your question, answered directly

The form also sends an email — will we get two leads?

Short answer: no today, and here is the check I ran plus the guard we still owe you. A single form submission can travel down three different roads into the CRM. Two of the three are already guarded by the code that has just been built. The third is currently a closed door — but it is unguarded, so it must be guarded before anyone opens it.

Verified on the live CRM database on 29 July 2026.
How a duplicate could happen What stops it Status
The website sends the same submission twice (retry after a timeout, visitor double-clicks) Every submission carries a unique ticket number. The second arrival is recognised and returns the original lead instead of creating a new one. LIVE
The same person submits a form twice this week, or submits after phoning The CRM looks for an open lead with the same phone number (or email) and the same name. If it finds one, the new enquiry is added to that lead's history instead of becoming a second lead. LIVE
Two different people share one phone number (family) Deliberately creates two leads, cross-referenced to each other, and flags them for a human. Merging two real people by accident is far worse than an extra row. LIVE
The notification email also creates a lead The CRM has four email addresses configured that turn incoming mail into leads. None of them can currently receive anything — there is no mail domain configured and no mailbox connected, and the form emails go to contact@pkgdvietuc.com and om@cstnvusaigon.com, which are not CRM addresses. So no duplicate exists today. The risk appears the day someone adds a CRM address to the form's recipients or connects those mailboxes to the CRM. GUARD NOT BUILT
Action — two small things, both cheap

1. Rule for the website developer (free): the form's notification email must go only to the staff mailboxes it goes to today. Never add a CRM address as a recipient, CC or BCC.

2. Rule for the CRM (small build, Phase W2): the notification email carries the same unique ticket number as the direct connection, in the email body and header. If a mailbox is ever connected to the CRM, any message carrying a ticket number that already exists is attached to that existing lead instead of creating a new one. That makes the third door safe to open whenever the business wants it.

For the website developer

Seven steps, in order

All work is on the WordPress side. Nothing from the CRM is installed into WordPress and the CRM never reaches into the website — the only connection is the website sending a message out to one address. Steps 1–3 deliver working leads; steps 4–7 add the reporting quality and legal cover.

Endpoint and credentials are already provisioned and waiting — ask the CRM team for the secret.
# Step Where What exactly Status
1 Build the relay plugin WordPress plugin (custom, small) Hook Contact Form 7's wpcf7_mail_sent event. For every submission: generate a unique ID, save a row in the plugin's own table, then POST the details to the CRM. If the CRM is unreachable, retry later using WP-Cron with the same ID — that is what makes retries safe. NOT STARTED
2 Store the credentials wp-config.php Client ID and secret as PHP constants — never in the WordPress database, never in a shared repository. The plugin exchanges them for a short-lived token at POST https://care.biztinct.com/oauth/token and caches it in a transient. NOT STARTED
3 Send to the CRM Plugin → CRM POST https://care.biztinct.com/api/v1/web/leads with the JSON below. The CRM replies created, merged, duplicate or rejected_spam — store the reply against the row so the WP admin list shows what happened. NOT STARTED
4 Remember the campaign context Google Tag Manager (GTM-P7S44SDQ) On landing, save utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_content, utm_term, gclid, fbclid, the landing address and the referring site into a first-party cookie (90 days). Read them back into hidden form fields at submit time. Meta's _fbp/_fbc and the Analytics client ID are read only if the visitor has accepted cookies. NOT STARTED
5 Add hidden fields + spam trap + consent box CF7 forms 15838, 15670, 15615 Hidden fields for everything in step 4; one hidden "honeypot" field that real people never fill; and a visible, unticked consent checkbox with versioned wording (final text pending legal review). The CRM stores what the visitor agreed to and when. NOT STARTED
6 Tag the click targets so the city is unambiguous Theme templates / widgets Both hotlines and both Zalo widgets appear on the same pages, so the page alone cannot tell us the city. Add data-city="hanoi" or data-city="hcmc" to every tel: link, Zalo widget and Facebook link. Then fire these events in GTM: generate_lead on successful form send, click_to_call, zalo_click, messenger_click — each with the city and the page. No names, phone numbers of visitors, message text or health wording may ever be put into these events. NOT STARTED
7 Prove it end to end, then reconcile daily Staging, then live Submit a test enquiry on staging and confirm the lead appears in the CRM with the right city and campaign. Once live, the plugin posts yesterday's ticket numbers to the CRM each morning and re-sends anything that never arrived. Any submission still unsent after all retries shows up in a WordPress admin list. NOT STARTED

Three rules that prevent painful mistakes

Always reuse the same submission_id when retrying — that is the entire duplicate defence. A new ID on a retry creates a second lead.
Save the submission locally before sending — if the send fails and nothing was saved, the enquiry is gone.
A spam rejection comes back as a success (rejected_spam), not an error. Mark it done and never retry it.

Making this sellable to other clinics

Turning the setup into a self-service connector

Right now the connection was switched on the way a developer does it: someone logged into the server, created a service account and generated a secret by hand. That is fine for one clinic. For a product sold to many clinics, each new customer must be able to connect their own website in a few minutes without a developer — the same way they already connect Zalo, Facebook or email in the CRM's Channel Connection Centre.

This is a new, small phase. Everything it needs already exists in the system — encrypted secret storage, connection health checks and an audit trail are all proven in the existing channel connectors.
